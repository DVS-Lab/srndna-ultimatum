## Behavioral results

The logistic mixed model included centered offer size, age group, partner
similarity, and all interactions, with participant-specific intercepts and
offer-size slopes. It converged without a singular fit in 47 participants
(4,438 responded human-partner trials). Acceptance increased with offer size
(beta=1.8013, SE=0.2373, z=7.59, p=3.17 × 10^-14). The Offer Size × Age Group ×
Partner Similarity interaction was not detected (beta=0.1136, SE=0.0947,
z=1.20, p=.230, 95% CI [-0.0720, 0.2991]). The uncertainty does not support
equivalence between age groups. A random-intercept-only sensitivity model
yielded the same inferential conclusion (beta=0.0684, SE=0.0915, p=.455).

## Corrected imaging results

Following correction of sub-144's event labels, affected first- and
second-level models were regenerated and the reported group covariates were
recomputed. The corrected default mode network analysis retained an age-group
difference in similar-minus-dissimilar offer-modulated connectivity: younger
adults exceeded older adults in a 29-voxel anterior cingulate cluster
(cluster-corrected p=.0138, Zmax=4.07, peak MNI [-13.3, 34.0, 27.8]; Figure 4).
The corrected executive control network fairness-sensitivity analysis yielded
no clusters surviving correction in any of its eight contrasts. The
previously reported positive ECN finding is therefore not retained.

The focal DMN analysis used FLAME 1+2 with a cluster-forming threshold of
Z>3.1 and whole-brain cluster-level family-wise error control at p<.05. Its
search mask contained 56,907 voxels; estimated FWHM was 7.72 × 7.75 × 7.40 mm
and DLH was 0.299801. FSL's reported `RESELS` value of 15.5628 is the size of
one resel in voxels, not the total number of resels in the search volume.

An analysis added during revision tested the equal-weighted mean of the
computer, age-similar, and age-dissimilar offer-size slopes. Five clusters
showed positive modulation (348 voxels total; maximum Z=4.75; cluster-corrected
p values from 2.56 × 10^-6 to .0453; Figure 3). No negative clusters survived.
This broad task-response result does not isolate perceived fairness from the
economic value of the offer.

## Exploratory partner ratings: methods and results

When an administration contained multiple complete attempts, the final
complete attempt was retained, applying the same curation rule across tasks.
First-attempt and repeat-session-exclusion analyses were retained as sensitivity
checks. Of the 47 imaging participants, 42 had pre-task and 42 had post-task
Ultimatum ratings; 41 had both. Missing ratings were not imputed.

The average of the two human partners was rated fairer than the computer
before the task (mean difference=1.060 rating points, t(41)=3.448, p=.00132,
FDR q=.00525) and afterward (difference=0.821, t(41)=3.215, p=.00255,
q=.00610). Human partners were also rated more likeable before
(difference=1.726, t(41)=5.500, p<.00001, q<.0001) and afterward
(difference=1.214, t(41)=3.724, p=.000591, q=.00284). FDR correction was
applied within the 24-test partner-contrast family. In contrast, no
similar-versus-dissimilar difference was detected in pre-task fairness or
likeability, or post-task fairness, likeability, anger, or satisfaction
(all six unadjusted p>=.243). These ratings do not directly measure perceived
similarity or belief in the partner cover story.

Pre-task rating differences between similar and dissimilar partners did not
detectably moderate the offer-by-similarity effect on acceptance in the
random-offer-slope models (42 participants, 3,969 trials): fairness
beta=0.0097, SE=0.0582, p=.868, 95% CI [-0.1045, 0.1238]; likeability
beta=-0.0099, SE=0.0475, p=.834, 95% CI [-0.1031, 0.0832]. Their corresponding
age interactions were not detected (p=.203 and .622). These tests do not
establish the absence of moderation.

## Optional supplementary ratings–sensitivity paragraph

The behavioral fairness-sensitivity score was not detectably correlated with
similar-minus-dissimilar fairness ratings (pre-task Pearson r=.093, p=.559;
post-task r=.032, p=.840). Exploratory rank correlations linked the score to
pre-task likeability (rho=-.405, FDR q=.037) and post-task anger (rho=.397,
q=.037), but the corresponding Pearson tests did not survive correction
(both q=.098). No age interaction survived correction across these
associations; the nominal pre-task likeability interaction was p=.043
(q=.298), not evidence of an established age-specific association. These
participant-score associations should not be conflated with the null direct
choice-moderation tests above.

## Transparency and scope

The correction changes the evidence supporting the submitted conclusions:
the DMN result is retained with updated statistics, whereas the ECN positive
claim is withdrawn. Task-wide activation and ratings analyses were added
during revision and are exploratory. Comparable observed choices and different
connectivity are co-occurring observations, not evidence of compensation or
preservation of behavior. The modest cross-sectional sample, selected-cluster
visualization, and absence of a direct perceived-similarity measure constrain
interpretation.
