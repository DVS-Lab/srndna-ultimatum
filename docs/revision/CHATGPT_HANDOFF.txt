RATINGS: COVERAGE, FINDINGS, AND LIMITS

The approved selection rule across tasks is the final complete attempt when
ratings were repeated. The earlier provisional repeat-selection dispute is
resolved. The paper subsample has 42 pre-task and 42 post-task administrations,
41 paired participants, and 43 with either timepoint. Sub-143 has no ratings
source. Sub-144's ratings have distinct participant-consistent provenance.
Missing ratings were not imputed. The 84 selected administrations (756 item
responses) matched the prepared BIDS behavior exports exactly.

Humans were rated fairer and more likeable than the computer before and after
the task (FDR q<.0062). Similar versus dissimilar humans did NOT differ
detectably on the six trait/timepoint contrasts (unadjusted p>=.243). These
ratings are not a direct test that participants believed the similarity
manipulation. Do not conflate human/computer and similar/dissimilar effects.

Direct choice moderation by pre-task ratings was not detected: fairness
p=.868 and likeability p=.834; age-interaction p=.203 and .622. These analyses
used 42 participants and 3,969 trials. Behavioral fairness sensitivity was
not detectably correlated with fairness ratings. Exploratory rank associations
with pre-task likeability and post-task anger survived FDR (q=.037 each), but
the Pearson versions did not (q=.098 each). This is qualified subsample
evidence, not proof of a choice mechanism or absence of an age interaction.
